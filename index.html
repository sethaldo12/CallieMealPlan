Weekly average: 1,529 calories — 154 g protein, 46 g fat, 125 g carbs, 28 g fiber. That's a 471-calorie daily deficit against a 2,000 maintenance, or a little under 1 lb of fat a week.

Also daily: 100 oz water, 8,000+ steps, and a hard floor of 1,400 calories — the rest-day number is the floor, so never go under it.

What the lifting is actually for

In a deficit without resistance training, roughly a quarter of the weight lost comes off as lean mass — you end up smaller but the same shape. With three sessions a week and 150 g of protein, that drops to near zero, and for someone new to lifting a couple of pounds of muscle usually go the other way. Why calories cycle now

The 300-calorie difference between day types is almost all carbohydrate, and it lands on the three days you train — so you have fuel for the session and material to recover with. The four rest days take the bigger cut. Same weekly deficit as a flat 1,529 every day, better sessions.

The week

Seven days, repeated for eight weeks. Everything is gluten free. Tap a day for its meals.

Weekly average: 1,529 calories — 154 g protein, 46 g fat, 125 g carbs, 28 g fiber.

Workouts

Three full-body sessions a week, each with a different emphasis. About 45–55 minutes including warm-up.

How to progress

The plan works only if the weight on the bar goes up. Everything else is detail.

  • Week 1, pick weights you could do 2–3 more reps with. Nowhere near failure. You're learning the movements and finding your starting numbers.
  • Every week, add a rep or add weight on at least one set of each lift. That's the whole progression model.
  • When you hit the top of the rep range on all three sets, add 5 lb on upper-body lifts or 10 lb on lower-body lifts, and drop back to the bottom of the range.
  • Write it down. A notes app is fine. Without a log there is no progression, only guessing.
  • Rest 90 seconds after the first two lifts of a session, 60 after the rest.
  • If a lift goes backwards two sessions running, that's a food and sleep signal, not a training one. Check both before changing the program.

The other four days

  • 8,000+ steps every day, training or not. This is the cheapest part of the deficit and it costs no food.
  • One easy 20–30 minute walk or bike on Saturday or Sunday if you want it. Optional.
  • Don't add cardio to burn more. The deficit is already set by the food. Cardio here is for health and steps, and adding a lot of it in a deficit mostly costs recovery.
Expect this in week 1

Soreness for two or three days after the first couple of sessions, and a 1–2 lb jump on the scale from muscle holding water. Both are normal, both pass, and neither means the plan isn't working. Don't react to either.

Before you start

Four things worth reading once, before the first grocery run.

The 9% body fat goal isn't one to build to

For a woman, roughly 10–13% is essential fat: the fat in bone marrow, the nervous system, and around the organs. 9% sits below that floor. Held for any length of time it's the territory of lost periods, dropping estrogen, and bone density loss that doesn't reliably come back. Female physique competitors touch 10–12% for a stage day and don't stay there.

This plan targets 19–21% — a visibly lean, athletic composition she can hold year-round. Eight weeks with lifting should land near 20–21%, which is the goal, not a step toward it.

The scale's body fat number is unusable

13.8% doesn't square with a BMI of 22.3, and bioimpedance scales run 5–8 points off on women. Use the scale for weight trend only. Track composition with waist and hip tape measurements, photos in the same light every two weeks, and how clothes fit — which matters more now, because lifting changes shape faster than it changes weight.

147 → 125 lb is a five-month project

Not an eight-week one. At this deficit she loses a little under 1 lb a week — roughly 7.5 lb over eight weeks, landing near 140 lb. Add two pounds of new muscle and the scale reads about 141 while the tape reads considerably smaller. The tape is the one to believe.

Is the gluten issue celiac, or a sensitivity?

It changes how strict this has to be. If celiac, add cross-contamination rules on top of the ingredient swaps: a dedicated toaster, separate cutting board and colander, her own jar of anything that gets double-dipped, and calling restaurants ahead. If it's a sensitivity or preference, reading labels is enough.

147 lb at 5′8″ is squarely in the healthy range, so there's no medical clock on any of this. If energy, mood, or strength drop off for more than a few days, that's the signal to ease up rather than push.

The rules

If everything else gets forgotten, these decide whether eight weeks works.

  • Protein floor of 150 g, every day. Training days and rest days both. If a day falls apart, hit the protein anyway — it's what decides whether you finish lean or just smaller.
  • Three sessions, and the weight goes up. Missing a session occasionally is fine. Doing the same weights for eight weeks is not — that's where the muscle-retention benefit comes from.
  • Fiber goes up gradually. Jumping to 28 g overnight causes bloating that gets blamed on the diet. Ramp over 4–5 days and keep the water high.
  • Weigh food for the first two weeks. Eyeballing drifts 20–30% high; after two weeks the eye is calibrated.
  • Weigh in daily, read weekly — morning, post-bathroom, pre-food. Only ever look at the 7-day average, and expect it to lag once lifting starts.
  • 8,000+ steps a day. The cheapest part of the deficit, and it costs no food.
  • 100 oz of water. Half the "starving" signal on a cut is thirst, and fiber needs the fluid to work.
  • Never below 1,400 calories. That's the rest-day number, so it's already the floor.
  • Alcohol counts — roughly 150 cal per drink, it comes out of Saturday, and beer is not gluten free.
