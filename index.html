Before you start

Four things worth reading once, before the first grocery run.

The 9% body fat goal isn't one to build to

For a woman, roughly 10–13% is essential fat: the fat in bone marrow, the nervous system, and around the organs. 9% sits below that floor. Held for any length of time it's the territory of lost periods, dropping estrogen, and bone density loss that doesn't reliably come back. Female physique competitors touch 10–12% for a stage day and don't stay there.

This plan targets 19–21% — a visibly lean, athletic composition she can hold year-round. If her true body fat is around 25%, six weeks here puts her near 21%: essentially the whole goal in one block. If she wants to go further later, 16–18% is the aggressive end, and it should be time-limited and watched.

The scale's body fat number is unusable

13.8% doesn't square with a BMI of 22.3, and bioimpedance scales run 5–8 points off on women. Use the scale for weight trend only. Track composition with waist and hip tape measurements, photos in the same light every two weeks, and how clothes fit.

147 → 125 lb is a five-month project

Not a six-week one. At a 500-calorie deficit she loses about 1 lb a week — roughly 6 lb in six weeks, landing near 141. Adding 2,000–3,000 daily steps widens that to about 7 lb without touching the food.

Is the gluten issue celiac, or a sensitivity?

It changes how strict this has to be. If celiac, add cross-contamination rules on top of the ingredient swaps: a dedicated toaster, separate cutting board and colander, her own jar of anything that gets double-dipped, and calling restaurants ahead. If it's a sensitivity or preference, reading labels is enough.

147 lb at 5′8″ is squarely in the healthy range, so there's no medical clock on any of this. If energy, mood, or strength drop off for more than a few days, that's the signal to ease up rather than push.

The rules that matter

If everything else gets forgotten, these are the ones that decide whether six weeks works.

  • Protein floor of 150 g. If a day falls apart, hit the protein anyway.
  • Fiber goes up gradually. Jumping from a low-fiber diet to 28 g overnight causes bloating and gas that gets blamed on the diet. Ramp over 4–5 days and keep the water high.
  • Weigh food for the first two weeks. Eyeballing drifts 20–30% high; after two weeks the eye is calibrated.
  • Weigh in daily, same conditions — morning, post-bathroom, pre-food. Only ever look at the 7-day average.
  • 8,000+ steps a day. At lightly active this is the cheapest deficit she has, and it doesn't cost her any food.
  • 100 oz of water. Half of the "starving" signal on a cut is thirst, and fiber needs the fluid to work.
  • Never below 1,400 calories, regardless of what the scale is doing.
  • Alcohol counts — roughly 150 cal per drink, it comes out of Saturday's flex meal rather than out of protein, and beer is not gluten free.
